Privacy in Allocute

Effective 17 November 2026. Allocute is published by Baltasar Jimenez Rincon, an independent developer in Spain.

A sentencing worksheet is client work. Allocute is built so that yours never leaves your iPhone unless you send it yourself.

Your cases

Case names, the guidelines you choose, the adjustments you mark, criminal history counts and statutory limits are stored in a file inside the app on your iPhone. They are not uploaded, synced or read by anyone. There is no account and Allocute has no server. Deleting a case removes it; deleting the app removes them all.

When you export a worksheet as a PDF, the file is written on the device and handed to the iOS share sheet. Where it goes from there (mail, Files, a printer) is your choice and happens outside Allocute.

The manual

The Guidelines Manual is inside the app. Reading and searching it works offline and sends nothing.

Purchases

The App Store handles payment; Allocute never sees your name, email or card. To know that a purchase happened, the app includes the RevenueCat SDK, which receives from the App Store the product, the date, the country and a random identifier made for this app. RevenueCat's policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

A few counters

Along with the purchase record, Allocute sends RevenueCat a handful of numbers about its own screens: how many times the Pro page was opened and closed, the name of the part of the app it was opened from (for example "second_case"), and which app version asked for a rating. They are tied to the same random identifier, not to you, and are used only to see whether the Pro page is shown at a sensible moment. Nothing about a case is ever among them. These counters are not sent from TestFlight builds.

What is absent

No advertising, no tracking across apps or sites, no third-party analytics beyond the above, no contacts, location, camera or microphone.

Children

Allocute is a professional reference tool and is not directed to children.
